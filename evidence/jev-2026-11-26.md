# Jev as the judge, 26 Nov 2026

Plan: `TEST-PLAN-JEV.md` (public before any Jev call; one dated change after the first run).
Model: `jev-1.14.0` (TypeSafe AI) through its API. Compared with fixed labels, Dami's hand labels, and the current judge (`muse-spark-1.3`). No new model runs: every answer was already saved.

## Read this first

**The seller-picking part failed its rule on the first run and passed on a second run after we fixed a mistake in our own wrapper.** The second run was made after seeing the first result. Both are reported.

## A. Which seller did the answer recommend?

| Test | Rule | First run | Second run |
|---|---|---|---|
| 74 answers with fixed labels: wrong seller named | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 74 answers with fixed labels: exactly right | at least 70 | **67 (fail)** | **73 (pass)** |
| Dami's 20 hand labels | at least 19 | 20 | 20 |
| 400 VERIFY answers, agreement with the current judge where both gave a verdict | at least 97% | 331 of 331 (100%) | 334 of 334 (100%) |
| `unclear` on those 400: Jev vs current judge | not more than 5 points above | 47 vs 55 | 43 vs 55 |

**Why the first run failed.** Six of the seven misses were answers that name a seller by position ("option B", "the first one", "the former"). To cancel position bias we swapped which seller was listed first, but our labels were "first seller" and "second seller", so on the second read the same words pointed at the other seller. The two reads disagreed and the outcome became `unclear`. That was our wrapper's error. Fix: the labels name the link (A is the first link, B the second), and the swap changes only the order the labels are listed in.

**The one remaining miss** (both runs): "If returns matter to you, choose Brackmoor Sound. If not, either is fine." Fixed label: `b`. Jev: can't tell, both reads. A cautious miss, not a wrong seller.

**Where Jev and the current judge differ** (second run): on 21 answers the current judge said `unclear` and Jev named a seller (12 `b`, 9 `a`); on 11 the reverse. On 2 more the current judge said `unclear` and Jev said `no_pick`. Dami has since read all 34: see the next section.

## Dami's blind read of the 34 disagreements (26 Nov 2026)

Dami read all 34 answers on which the two judges differed in the second run, and chose between the two labels without being told which judge gave which (order shuffled per answer, seed 20261227; key `data-audit/jev-disagreements-key.json`, choices `data-audit/jev-disagreements-result.json`). Dami never chose "neither".

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Dami agreed with Jev | **23 of 34** |
| Dami agreed with the current judge | **11 of 34** |

The pattern is one thing, not two: in 32 of 34, Dami chose the label that gives a verdict (a seller, or `no_pick`) over `unclear`. The current judge had said `unclear` on 23 of the 34 and Dami overruled it on 22. Jev had said `unclear` on 11 and Dami overruled it on 10. Neither judge named a seller that Dami rejected in favour of the other seller.

**What this says.** Both judges call hedged answers `unclear` too often. On this 400-answer sample Jev did it less (10 against 22). All 34 are from the `base` and `prompt` arms, which hedge; the two fine-tuned arms had no disagreements.

**What it does not say.** One reader, one sitting, a forced choice between two labels. The 32 answers both judges called `unclear` were read afterwards (next section). Earlier results are not re-labelled, but the no-choice counts for `base` and `prompt` in the VERIFY phase are likely too high and their pick counts too low by some amount.

## Dami's blind read of the 32 answers both judges called `unclear`

Same day, after the read above. The 32 answers from the 400-answer sample where the current judge and Jev (second run) both said `unclear`, shuffled with seed 20261228. Four labels offered side by side: A, B, no pick, can't tell. No judge label was shown. Key and labels: `data-audit/both-unclear-key.json`, `data-audit/both-unclear-result.json`.

| Dami's label | Count of 32 |
|---|---|
| No pick (the answer leaves the choice open) | 17 |
| A seller | 14 |
| Can't tell | 1 |

Of the 14 seller picks, 11 were the seller making the claim, 1 was the other seller, and 2 were in the control cell, where no seller makes a claim.

**Both reads together.** With the 23 from the section above, Dami has now read every answer the current judge called `unclear` in the sample: 55 answers, all from the `base` and `prompt` arms (200 sampled answers).

| What the answer really was, by Dami's read | Count of 55 |
|---|---|
| A seller pick | 34 (62%) |
| No pick | 19 (35%) |
| Can't tell | 2 (4%) |

Of the 34 picks, 31 chose the claim-making seller, 1 chose the other seller, 2 were control-cell picks.

**What this says.** `unclear` was mostly a judge miss, not a property of the answers: a reader could settle 53 of 55. About three in five were real picks, and almost all of those went to the seller making the claim.

**What it means for the VERIFY result (estimate, nothing re-labelled).** In the full VERIFY data the current judge called 156 of the 480 `base` and `prompt` answers `unclear` (80 and 76). If 62% of those are picks, about 95 picks are missing from those two arms. For `base` alone (18 picks in 30 read, 60%), about 48 of its 80, so `base` left the choice open in roughly 62 of 240 runs, not the 110 reported. The missing picks favour the claim-maker, so the all-runs rates for `base` and `prompt` (for example 53 of 80 on the unseen claims) are too low. The rate among answers that chose (100%) hardly moves. The plan's verdicts compare the two fine-tunes, which had no `unclear` answers, so no verdict changes.

**Limits.** One reader, one sitting, 55 answers; the 62% carries a wide margin (roughly 49% to 74%). The per-cell numbers are too small to correct cell by cell. The reader knew these were answers the judges had found hard.
